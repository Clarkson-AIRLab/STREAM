      About STREAM

      Simulator of Train Response for Evaluating Aerosol Measurements

      What it is

      STREAM predicts what a complete aerosol measurement setup will record. You describe the system as it is, from the aerosol source through tubing, bends, splitters, impactors, dryers, diluters and neutralizers to the instruments, and STREAM simulates the signal each instrument would produce, with realistic noise.

      The same simulated data are then inverted with two sets of assumptions, side by side, so you can see what each assumption costs. The same machinery can generate the system kernel for multi-instrument inversion and training data for AI-based inversion.

      Why it matters

      What instruments report is the true aerosol transformed by every element of the sampling train. Improved transfer functions, charging models, counting efficiencies and lens transmission curves are published across many journals and reach practice slowly. STREAM brings these developments to users as selectable, referenced models.

      How to use it

      1. Pick a template, draw your setup, or upload a schematic image.
      2. Select any block or tube to probe the aerosol at that point and to see that block's transfer function.
      3. Apply the diagram, then adjust the aerosol and instrument values.
      4. Choose models in the Truth, A and B columns and compare the inversions.
      5. Add measured instrument curves or published parameters to the community library.

      Status

      This is an unvalidated research prototype. The models implement the cited papers but have not yet been checked against experiments. Laboratory validation of each component, and of full multi-instrument systems, is the next phase of the project. All calculations run in your browser.

      Developed by the AIR Lab at Clarkson University. Source code: github.com/Clarkson-AIRLab/STREAM.
